Competitive field

Dated research findings and the evidence that limits them

The August research found established approaches on several axes: temporary access to a role or resource, approval of a platform-specific operation, transaction signing, and portable signed evidence. These are useful comparison dimensions. A search that combines them into one narrow category can overlook a substitute that solves the buyer's problem differently.

Concrete public examples remain available. Teleport documents configurable approver counts for temporary access and automatic reviews. Vault documents additional authorizations before a protected request is satisfied. GitHub deployment environments can require a review, prevent self-review and delay access to environment secrets. The versioned in-toto specification defines thresholded steps and verifiable supply-chain records. These examples establish existing components and controls, not equivalence to every MPAS property. See primary comparison references.

The dated conclusion is a narrower interoperability hypothesis: keep an approval attached to an exact action across systems and make its evidence portable. The research did not prove an empty market or the absence of a competitor with the full combination. The searches were bounded; their counts were evidence floors rather than a census.

The adverse product signal is equally useful. An automatic review can remove delay, while per-action human review can add queues and fatigue. The existence of automatic-review products is counterevidence to assuming every buyer wants an additional manual approval. A withdrawn approval feature, by itself, cannot distinguish weak demand from integration cost or a changed product strategy. No market refresh or demand measurement is claimed here.

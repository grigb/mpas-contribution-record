Research

Dated research conclusions, counterevidence and unanswered questions

The research narrowed the product claim to exact actions, policy-bound approvals, controlled execution and portable evidence. It also retained evidence against overbroad novelty, market and safety claims. The source chapters date from August 13, the seven-answer synthesis from August 28 and the executive and commercial reports from August 31. Later engineering acceptance is recorded separately.1

What the research changed

Product and market

What the dated research says about competing approaches, the potential advantage, prospective buyers and adoption choices.

  • Competitive field — Compares existing access, workflow and signed-record systems, including counterevidence to broad novelty and empty-market claims.
  • Differentiation and limits — Examines the proposed combination of controls and the narrower interoperability advantage that the evidence may support.
  • Buyers and pricing — Separates possible buyers, pricing units and market assumptions from verified demand or willingness to pay.
  • Product scope and adoption — Distinguishes the open protocol from operated products and compares the learning offered by different adoption paths.

Design and safety

How the protocol binds an exact action to approval, and where signer independence, external effects and privacy limit its claims.

  • Actions, approvals and evidence — Explains action packages, payload binding, verification, execution and receipts, including native and adapter-based integration.
  • Signers and agent boundaries — Explains why distinct identities need not provide independent judgment and what credential separation can and cannot establish.
  • External effects and privacy — Examines receipt meaning, uncertain remote outcomes and disclosure risks that approval and signed evidence alone do not remove.

Implementation evidence

What was considered buildable, what recorded tests and prototypes established, and how those findings differ from live operation.

  • Feasibility and test evidence — Separates implementable mechanisms and dated component-test results from integrated behavior, live-agent operation and deployment readiness.
  • Prototype exercises — Summarizes the recorded simulated exercises, retaining failed checks and limits on what those exercises demonstrate.

Standards and open questions

What the dated standards research can support, how the evidence was corrected, and which questions remain unresolved.

  • Liability, regulation and standards — Distinguishes historical legal and standards observations from current legal conclusions, certification, contribution terms and ownership agreements.
  • Research quality and open questions — Records corrections to overbroad claims and the remaining questions about demand, approvals, privacy, deployment and rights.

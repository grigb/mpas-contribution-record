Engineering

Action, policy, credential and execution protections with dated acceptance

The engineering work protects the action from untrusted input through approval, credential use, execution and outcome reporting. A proposer requests the action, signers approve or reject it, a verifier evaluates permission and a credential adapter performs operations for systems that do not verify MPAS directly.1

The two public merges and the private contribution program are different evidence. Local acceptance establishes the bounded repair in its recorded source. It does not establish a later upstream merge, live deployment or final integrated acceptance.2

The September 14 record contains 52 accepted component repairs and three written conformance entries with accepted partial offline evidence. Final integrated finding acceptance remained pending for all 55 entries. The improvements cover the complete governed path, but they do not establish accepted real-agent behavior or deployment.2

Engineering topics

Browse the groups below for the scope of the work, then open a topic for its full account.

Approval and permissions

How an action is read, bound to its approvals, checked against policy, and kept within the intended authority.

  • Untrusted input — Rejects ambiguous structured input so different components do not interpret the same request differently.
  • Actions and signatures — Checks the intended payload, eligible signatures, expiry and conflicting decisions before accepting approval.
  • Approval policies — Preserves nested policy meaning, distinct-signer requirements and detection of requirements that cannot be satisfied.
  • Signer authority — Defines what the signer reviews and keeps displayed authority metadata from granting credential access.

Execution and recovery

How requests recover from failures without losing their state or repeating an action whose result is uncertain.

  • Temporary failures — Keeps temporary policy failures distinct from final decisions and preserves the request state needed for recovery.
  • Duplicate prevention — Coordinates dispatch and recovery to prevent repeated actions while retaining uncertain outcomes and the recorded durability limits.

Credentials and results

How access to external systems, action receipts and returned information stay within the permitted scope.

  • Sign-in and transport — Constrains delegated sign-in, token refresh and transport behavior without silently widening permission or repeating an operation.
  • Receipts — Checks receipts against the approved action and expected issuer; an issuer's statement does not prove every external effect.
  • Tools and returned information — Validates generated-tool inputs and controls which returned information reaches an agent.

Integration and delivery

How the changes reach applications and reviewable deliveries, with public merges distinguished from private acceptance.

  • Applications and setup — Carries authority changes into applications, adapters and developer setup, retaining the limits of offline and synthetic-provider checks.
  • Merged changes — Explains the two public fixes for distinct-signer counting and ambiguous signed approval data, without implying deployment.
  • Later repairs and delivery — Describes later repairs and the combined private candidate, with package dependencies and acceptance limits.
  • Review and presentation material — Maps the available reports, presentations and contribution-review material, including the separately unfinished final technical report.

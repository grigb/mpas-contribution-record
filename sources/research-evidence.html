Research evidence

Dated conclusions, counterevidence and selected primary references

Dated research evidence summary

This public selection preserves conclusions from the August 13 subject reports, August 28 synthesis and August 31 executive reports. September engineering results are dated separately. It omits private application terms, commercial draft figures and identifying narratives. Public primary references checked on September 17 support the specific claims indicated; they do not turn the August corpus into a current market census. Prototype figures are retained evidence summaries with private underlying records, not independently inspectable public tests.

Exact actions, approvals and portable evidence

MPAS represents a requested action as an Action Package: the execution payload, an envelope that binds it by a cryptographic digest, and approvals. A proposer requests the action, signers review it and a deterministic verifier decides whether the package satisfies policy. Native verification and a credential adapter are alternative integration routes; the adapter is useful when the target does not verify MPAS directly.

The digest ties approval to the data that will run. Rechecking it can detect payload substitution; it does not establish that a display faithfully explained the action or that the action is wise. Authorization says what may happen. A receipt reports an outcome. Transporting either object does not by itself establish execution.

This is a summary of the research's August 31 specification reading. The public Core text is pinned to its cited revision; it describes Draft v0.2 and leaves approval counts to policy. See the pinned specification references.

Competitive field

The August research found established approaches on several axes: temporary access to a role or resource, approval of a platform-specific operation, transaction signing, and portable signed evidence. These are useful comparison dimensions. A search that combines them into one narrow category can overlook a substitute that solves the buyer's problem differently.

Concrete public examples remain available. Teleport documents configurable approver counts for temporary access and automatic reviews. Vault documents additional authorizations before a protected request is satisfied. GitHub deployment environments can require a review, prevent self-review and delay access to environment secrets. The versioned in-toto specification defines thresholded steps and verifiable supply-chain records. These examples establish existing components and controls, not equivalence to every MPAS property. See primary comparison references.

The dated conclusion is a narrower interoperability hypothesis: keep an approval attached to an exact action across systems and make its evidence portable. The research did not prove an empty market or the absence of a competitor with the full combination. The searches were bounded; their counts were evidence floors rather than a census.

The adverse product signal is equally useful. An automatic review can remove delay, while per-action human review can add queues and fatigue. The existence of automatic-review products is counterevidence to assuming every buyer wants an additional manual approval. A withdrawn approval feature, by itself, cannot distinguish weak demand from integration cost or a changed product strategy. No market refresh or demand measurement is claimed here.

Differentiation and its limits

The proposed value joins exact-action binding, eligible approvals, separation of credential custody from the requesting agent, constrained execution and portable evidence. The research treats this as a combination to demonstrate, not an invention claim for each component.

The pinned JSON policy profile excludes a proposer from satisfying a threshold for the same action, but also defines a proposer-only requirement. It is therefore inaccurate to claim that every conforming action must involve multiple parties. The actual policy and governed surface matter. See the policy reference.

Portable authorization and portable attestation answer different questions. A record that a workflow occurred does not necessarily authorize its next operation. The public in-toto example supplies an established attestation comparison; it is not evidence that all systems share the same action scope. The earlier MPAS distinct-signer counting defect is documented as merged on August 28, not repeated as an unresolved present defect.

Feasibility and engineering evidence

The feasibility work found that canonical serialization, signature binding, policy evaluation and dispatch control can be implemented with available technology. Buildability does not establish a production schedule, operating cost, safe deployment or customer value.

The evidence changed over time. Earlier August chapters were source readings. Later reports recorded executed tests. September records then accepted specific component repairs and offline conformance fixtures. An older statement that nothing had run cannot override a later recorded run; a later component pass cannot establish a missing system test.

The September 11 combined-source record reports 190 declared, executed and passing conformance fixtures inside a synthetic-provider, non-model boundary. The September 14 record still reports zero accepted real-agent scenarios out of 17 and pending final integrated finding acceptance. These are separate dated evidence units and are never added to a lifetime test total. See engineering evidence.

The useful feasibility distinction is between repairable implementation faults and limits that require a different trust model. A parser or signature check can be repaired locally. Independent approval judgment, uncertain remote execution and information inferred from allowed outputs require their own evidence and cannot be established by those repairs alone.

Security, signer independence and agent boundaries

A threshold counts accepted signatures. It cannot determine whether the signers share an operator, model, prompt, information source or incentive to approve the same bad action. Key and process isolation are useful controls; they are not measurements of independent judgment.

This limit is not unique to the project. The versioned in-toto specification explicitly scopes out colluding or deceived participants acting together and does not promise that a correctly followed process cannot introduce a vulnerability. That is an external comparison, not an MPAS experiment. See the in-toto reference.

The narrower containment claim is that a trusted component can hold a reusable credential and decide when to use it. The requesting agent can still cause permitted actions. Ungoverned routes around that component need separate control. The relevant questions are what the agent can reach, which destination and operation are permitted, and whether every effect passes the governed dispatch point.

The dated research did not execute colluding real approvers or a human-approval comprehension study. Simulated decisions do not establish how real people or models behave. This edition makes no numerical reliability claim for MPAS signers and no claim that adding signers prevents collusion.

External effects, receipt meaning and privacy

A receipt proves what the signer of that receipt attested. It does not independently prove every action taken by a remote counterparty. Stronger end-to-end evidence may require the executing system to sign its own statement and still depends on the trust assigned to that system.

An arbitrary external service cannot generally join an atomic transaction with the caller's local database. If an action was transmitted and the reply is lost, resending may duplicate it. The supported design is at-most-once dispatch with an honest indeterminate outcome when the external result cannot be resolved. It is not a universal exactly-once guarantee.

The Action Package can expose the identities and decisions of its signers. Portable evidence is not automatically private evidence. The research identified threshold signing as a possible future direction, while leaving its compatibility with eligible-signer policy and evidence requirements unresolved.

Research prototypes also showed that individually permitted results can leak information through their combination or through success-versus-failure outcomes. These are bounded observations about the named exercises, not measurements of every MPAS deployment. Content inspection alone should not be equated with complete prevention of inference or covert channels.

Buyers, price and commercial evidence

The August research corpus did not establish verified buyer interviews, paid pilots, pricing conversations or budget-holder commitments for the proposed combination. That describes the corpus at its cutoff. It is not a claim about every organization or any later relationship.

Possible users included platform builders, identity and security teams, financial operations, infrastructure teams and organizations that approve actions across trust boundaries. The operator of a control need not own its budget. An incumbent platform may already control purchasing and distribution, making an embedded component more plausible than a separate service.

The comparison must ask what is being bought: a permitted call, a reviewed action, an integration, evidence retention or operating responsibility. Seats, workloads, decisions and API calls are different units. Adjacent-market revenue does not become the addressable market for MPAS, and a search with no matching procurement result does not prove that demand is absent.

No observed price in the retained research established willingness to pay for this combination. A useful demand test would start from an actual consequential action and an inadequate existing control, then measure the value and cost of replacing it. That remains a research conclusion, not completed outreach, a selected price or a commercial commitment.

Product scope and adoption paths

A protocol defines how systems exchange actions, approvals and evidence. An operated product must also provide a usable review experience, integrations, reliable recovery, support and a reason to adopt it. Those are separate deliverables.

MPAS can govern a data-access request; that alone does not provide retrieval, row filtering, classification, secret storage or complete prevention of information leakage. Payment and deployment targets continue to own settlement, rollback and their execution controls.

These are research alternatives, not selected company plans. Two product hypotheses require different evidence: improve the action a human sees, or serve workloads where a human cannot keep pace. Latency, review quality, integration cost and a measured baseline help distinguish them. The research did not establish that either is the first product to build.

Liability, regulation and standards

The research separated several questions that are often collapsed: whether a protocol text is published, which approval process it has completed, whether software follows it, whether a deployment satisfies a requirement, and which party bears responsibility for an action. One kind of evidence does not answer all the others.

Approval evidence records assent; it does not make the approved act safe or settle responsibility for loss. A receipt and a source fingerprint likewise do not settle ownership, certification or contribution rights. This edition makes no legal, licensing, insurance-coverage or compliance conclusion for a person, company or deployment.

The pinned MPAS Core source describes itself as Draft v0.2. Its intended venue is not proof of completed standards adoption. RFC 9396, published in May 2023, is a public example of structured authorization details predating this research; it defines data for requesting authorization, not a mandate to deploy MPAS. See protocol and standards references.

The practical research result is to keep source status and force separate. Drafts, consultations, recommendations, operative requirements, implementation tests and certification decisions need their own dates and authorities. No current legal-status review was performed for this edition. Later offline conformance work is useful implementation evidence but does not create external certification or standards adoption.

Recorded prototype exercises

The retained August exercise review read existing code and result records rather than rerunning the exercises. All participants and service datasets were simulated. The following figures belong to separate runs and must not be summed as unique project tests.

Exercise and retained dateRecorded outcomeWhat it established and did not establish
Payment approval, August 190 checks passedA purpose-built local gateway and substitute provider; no live payment service and no execution of the main MPAS implementation
Two-party ceremony, August 1 and 423 checks passed in retained recordsA separate implementation kernel and simulated decisions; repeated runs do not add unique cases
Containment, reviewed August 1312 probes: 10 passed and 2 failedA local boundary model with retained escape and outcome-disclosure failures; not a production sandbox measurement

The payment exercise observed whether a denied action actually reached the substitute provider. Positive controls established permitted execution. Guard-removal and mutation controls tested whether rejection depended on the intended protection. Denial messages alone would have been weaker evidence.

The ceremony retained rejection, expiry, changed-payload, replay and uncertain-execution cases. A timeout did not self-approve; a lost or unqueryable result did not trigger an automatic second action. These outcomes belong to the separate exercise, not every MPAS implementation.

The containment exercise retained both failed controls: a boundary escape and information disclosed through outcomes. The knowledge-access exercise retained a sensitive inference formed by combining individually permitted answers. A deliberately leaky control demonstrated detection, while an absent required observer invalidated a result even when no leak was seen. These adverse results rule out a claim of complete containment or general semantic-disclosure protection. Unpublished reproduction instructions remain outside this edition.

Research quality and unresolved evidence

The research corrected overbroad novelty, adoption, implementation and test claims. It distinguished a source reading, a retained historical run, a newly executed test and an independently accepted result. Negative findings remain useful when later work repairs a defect; a later repair changes the dated state without erasing the original evidence.

The open questions are concrete: actual demand and willingness to pay; whether reviewers understand the exact action; whether real signers fail independently or collude; what allowed outputs reveal in combination; how a deployment handles remote uncertainty; and whether the delivered implementation passes its required integrated and real-agent scenarios.

The public primary references establish selected protocol rules and existing approaches. The local source summaries establish what the retained research and engineering records say. They do not expose the underlying private tests or convert an unperformed experiment into evidence. This edition states both types so a reader can judge each claim at the right level.
